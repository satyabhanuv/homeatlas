## LinkedIn post (longer, professional)

I've been quietly building Nearnity for the last year on nights and weekends, and I'm at the point where I need real people to push on it before I open it up publicly.

**The problem I wanted to solve:** every time I needed to find something local — the nearest urgent care, my city's voter registration portal, where to recycle paint, what's happening at the library this weekend — I'd end up on five different ad-heavy sites, each one trying to convert me into a lead. Public information that local governments publish for free, gated behind ads.

**What Nearnity does:** one clean page per address. Free clinics from the federal HRSA registry. Schools from federal NCES. Climate risks from FEMA, Cal Fire, NOAA. Events from Ticketmaster, SeatGeek, city iCal feeds. Hospitals from Medicare Care Compare. State-licensed contractors from CSLB. Business licenses from city open-data portals. All free, source-linked, no ads, no accounts, no tracking.

## Tips for the rollout

1. **Post LinkedIn first** — that's where your professional network sits and they'll give thoughtful feedback. Wait an hour, then post Instagram.
2. **Pin the LinkedIn post** to your profile until launch day so anyone who lands on your profile sees it.
3. **DM 5 people directly** with the short version above — DMs convert way better than feed posts for "please test my thing" asks.
4. **First 48 hours matter most** — that's when feedback is freshest. Check your inbox + the Worker KV (`nearnity:feedback:v1:index`) every few hours.
5. **Don't argue with feedback** — even when it's wrong. Just thank them and tag in your followup list. People stop sending feedback the moment they feel their last note was contested.
6. **Reply within 24 hours** with at least a "got it, thanks — this is in the v2.8 list" — even one sentence. People remember being heard.
