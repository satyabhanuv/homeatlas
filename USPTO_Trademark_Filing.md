# USPTO Trademark Filing for "Nearnity"

Step-by-step filing of a federal trademark for **NEARNITY** as a wordmark + logo, in International Class 042 (online software services).

**Estimated total time:** 90 minutes of your active work, then 6-12 months of USPTO examination.

**Total cost:** **$250** for TEAS Plus filing in one class. ($350 if you slip into TEAS Standard tier.)

**What you'll get:** Federal registration of NEARNITY as a trademark. Gives you exclusive nationwide rights to use the name for the goods/services described, plus the right to use ® after the name.

---

## Pre-filing checklist (do these BEFORE you start the application)

### 1. Confirm the name is still clean (5 min)

You did a basic USPTO search earlier and got "clean." Re-confirm:

1. Open https://tmsearch.uspto.gov
2. Click **Basic Word Mark Search (new)**
3. Search term: `Nearnity`
4. Search field: `Combined Word Mark`
5. Click Submit. You should see **0 results** or only old dead marks.
6. Also search variants: `nearnity`, `nearinity` (common misspellings), `nearntiy`.

If anything live and confusingly similar appears in classes 035 (advertising / business services) or 042 (computer / software services), STOP and talk to a trademark attorney before filing. Otherwise, proceed.

### 2. Decide what to file (10 min — important decision)

You have three options:

| Filing type | What's protected | Cost | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wordmark only** ("NEARNITY") | Just the word in any font | $250 | ✅ Start here |
| **Design mark only** (the logo SVG) | Just the visual mark | $250 | Skip — protect the word first |
| **Both** (filed separately) | Both | $500 | Eventually, but not today |

**Recommendation: file the wordmark only.** Word marks are stronger and broader than design marks — they protect the name in any font/style/layout. If someone later copies your logo design, the wordmark gives you a strong basis. You can always add a design mark filing later.

### 3. Pick the right International Class (5 min)

Nearnity is a free public-data aggregator delivered as a website. The right primary class is:

**Class 042** — "Computer and scientific services" — specifically: *"Providing temporary use of non-downloadable software for accessing public-data information about specific addresses."*

You could ALSO file in Class 035 (advertising / business / data compilation services), but for now Class 042 alone is the strongest protection and avoids the cost of a second class.

### 4. Gather what you need (10 min)

Open a notes file and gather these — you'll paste them into the form:

```
TRADEMARK: NEARNITY

OWNER NAME (entity): Satya Bhanu Velivela
  OR if your LLC is filed: Nearnity LLC (a California Limited Liability Company)
  → IMPORTANT: file in the entity that will actually use the mark long-term.
    If your LLC will be filed in the next 2 weeks, WAIT and file under the LLC.
    If filing in your personal name now, you can assign to LLC later — but that's an extra $40 + paperwork.

OWNER ADDRESS: [your personal mailing address — NOT a P.O. Box]
OWNER CITIZENSHIP / STATE: United States, California

EMAIL: svelivela@paypal.com  (USPTO will send all official correspondence here)

CLASS: 042

GOODS / SERVICES IDENTIFICATION (must use USPTO-approved language for TEAS Plus):
  "Providing temporary use of non-downloadable software for accessing
  geographically-localized public-data information; software-as-a-service
  (SaaS) services featuring software for aggregating public records from
  government and civic sources."

  → You'll pick exact language from USPTO's "ID Manual" during the form.
  → If you write your own description, you fall into TEAS Standard tier
    (+$100). Stick with ID Manual entries to stay TEAS Plus at $250.

DATE OF FIRST USE IN COMMERCE: [date of public launch — July 13, 2026]
DATE OF FIRST USE ANYWHERE: [date the site first went live — earlier, May 2026]
  → If you haven't publicly launched yet by July 13, file as INTENT TO USE (1B).
    1B filings are valid and reserve your name for 6 months while you launch.
    Cost is the same $250.

SPECIMEN OF USE (proof of actual use in commerce):
  → A screenshot of nearnity.com showing the NEARNITY wordmark with the
    services description visible on the page. Save as PNG, 800×600+.
    File path: /Personal/Nearnity_USPTO_specimen.png
  → Capture this AFTER public launch. If filing Intent to Use (1B), you
    don't need a specimen yet — submit later with $100 Statement of Use.
```

### 5. Decide: file now (Intent to Use) or wait until after public launch (Use in Commerce)?

This is a strategy decision.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **File 1B (Intent to Use) now** | Lock in the priority date earlier — important if someone tries to register Nearnity in the meantime | Requires a $100 Statement of Use filing after launch |
| **File 1A (Use in Commerce) after July 13 public launch** | One filing, one fee | Slightly later priority date |

**My recommendation:** **File 1A (Use in Commerce) the week of July 14**, immediately after public launch. The risk of someone else filing for "Nearnity" in those ~3 weeks between now and July 13 is essentially zero (it's a unique made-up word). And waiting saves you the $100 follow-up.

UNLESS you're filing in your LLC's name and want to file BEFORE LLC formation completes — in which case file as the LLC under 1B intent-to-use after LLC paperwork is done.

## Filing the application — TEAS Plus

### Step 1 — Open the USPTO TEAS Plus form (5 min)

1. Go to https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/apply
2. Click **File a Trademark Application** → **TEAS Plus**.
3. You'll be prompted to log in. Create an account if you don't have one:
   - **MyUSPTO** account at https://my.uspto.gov
   - Use the same email you'll list as the correspondence email.

### Step 2 — Application form section by section (45 min)

The form is long but straightforward. Save progress as you go (USPTO lets you save and return for 60 days).

#### Section A: Applicant Information
- Type of entity: **Individual** (if filing personally) or **Limited Liability Company** (if filed under LLC).
- For LLC: enter LLC formation date + state (CA).
- Address: your mailing address (must be a street address, not P.O. Box).
- Citizenship: United States; State: California.

#### Section B: Mark Information
- Mark type: **Standard Character Mark** (this is the wordmark; protects in any font).
- Mark: `NEARNITY` (all caps is convention — protects upper/lower case in actual use).
- DO NOT upload an image. Standard Character protects the word, not a specific design.

#### Section C: Goods and Services
- Class: 042
- Click **Pick from ID Manual** (this is what keeps you in TEAS Plus tier).
- Search the ID Manual for:
  - `software as a service`
  - `non-downloadable software`
- Recommended entry to select:
  > "Software as a service (SAAS) services featuring software for providing aggregated public-data information about specific geographic addresses, including events, healthcare facilities, schools, and emergency services."
- If the exact wording isn't in the ID Manual, pick the closest one and proceed. USPTO will sometimes accept ID Manual entries with minor tweaks.

#### Section D: Filing Basis
- For each class, pick:
  - **1A — Use in Commerce** (recommended, if filing after July 13)
  - **1B — Intent to Use** (if filing before public launch)

For 1A:
- Date of First Use Anywhere: `MM/DD/YYYY` (when you first showed nearnity.com publicly — even to testers)
- Date of First Use in Commerce: `MM/DD/YYYY` (public launch date)
- **Upload Specimen of Use:** PNG/JPG of nearnity.com homepage clearly showing the NEARNITY wordmark + the services offered. Must be 800×600 or larger.
- **Specimen description:** "Screenshot of applicant's website at nearnity.com showing the mark in use with the recited services."

#### Section E: Correspondence
- Designated email: svelivela@paypal.com (USPTO sends ALL correspondence here including office actions — must check this email weekly).
- Domestic representative: not required if applicant is in USA.

#### Section F: Fee Information
- Fee: **$250 per class** (you have 1 class) = **$250 total**.
- Pay via credit card or USPTO deposit account.

#### Section G: Signature
- Sign electronically with your name.
- Statement: "The signatory believes that the applicant is the owner of the trademark/service mark sought to be registered, [...]"
- Read and check all the standard verification boxes.

### Step 3 — Submit (1 min)

- Review the summary page CAREFULLY.
- Click Submit.
- **Save the confirmation page as PDF** — it contains your Serial Number (e.g., 99/123,456). You'll need this for all future correspondence.
- Pay the $250 fee.

USPTO will send a confirmation email within minutes.

## What happens next (6-12 months)

| Stage | Typical timing | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Filing acknowledged | Same day | Confirmation email with serial number |
| Examining Attorney assigned | 3-4 months from filing | A USPTO attorney reviews the application |
| First Office Action (if any) | 3-5 months from filing | They may request clarifications, ID manual fixes, or specimen issues. You have **6 months to respond** (free) or **3 months** for some types of responses |
| Publication for Opposition | 4-6 months from filing | The application appears in the USPTO's Trademark Official Gazette for 30 days. Anyone can oppose; for an unused name like NEARNITY, opposition is rare |
| Registration | 6-10 months from filing | If no opposition, you receive the registration certificate. You can now use ® |

**Check your status regularly:** https://tsdr.uspto.gov → enter your serial number.

---

## After registration

1. **Use ® after the name** in marketing and on the site footer. Example: "Nearnity®" or "Nearnity® — source-linked public data".
2. **Renew at year 5-6** ($225) by filing a Section 8 Declaration of Use.
3. **Renew at year 10** ($425) and every 10 years thereafter.
4. **Police your mark** — if you see someone using NEARNITY in a confusing way, you can send a cease-and-desist (often a $200-500 attorney-drafted letter is enough).

## Common pitfalls to avoid

- **Don't file in two classes if one will do.** Each class is +$250. Class 042 covers your launch service.
- **Don't write your own goods/services description.** Use the ID Manual. Custom descriptions drop you to TEAS Standard at +$100 per class.
- **Don't list a P.O. Box as your address.** USPTO requires a physical address.
- **Don't miss the email.** Examining Attorneys send office actions by email; if you miss the 6-month response window, your application is abandoned and you forfeit the $250.
- **Don't file under your LLC if the LLC isn't formed yet.** The LLC must legally exist at the time of filing.
- **Don't skip the specimen if filing 1A.** A bare screenshot of nearnity.com is fine, but it must clearly show NEARNITY being used to identify the service (e.g., the logo + the services described on the page).

## When you'd want a trademark attorney instead

You can absolutely file this yourself for $250. Hire an attorney IF:
- You get an Office Action you don't understand and the deadline is approaching.
- The Examining Attorney rejects on "likelihood of confusion" with another mark you didn't find in your search.
- Someone opposes your publication during the 30-day Gazette window.
- You want to expand into multiple classes simultaneously (035 + 042 + 009).

Trademark attorney for an Office Action response: $400-1000 flat fee.
Trademark attorney to handle the entire filing for you: $800-1500 + USPTO fee.
